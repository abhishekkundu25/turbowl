# Verification: loop iteration 4, dictionary slots to three quarters (2026-11-06)

The change: the dictionary grows its hash slots when they pass three quarters
full instead of half, and every way of filling it reaches the same size
(`d21803b`). The kept version before it is `eebb962` (iteration 3). The
decision and the reasoning are iteration 4's row in `docs/specs/experiments.md`.

Raw results are in [`data/2026-11-06-e04/`](data/2026-11-06-e04/).

## Correctness

Every suite passed on the merged tree (`d21803b`): the core library (316
tests, two new ones bounding the slots' fill and checking that every way of
filling sizes them alike) and its doc tests; the CLI; the oracle library and
`minimal`, `programs`, `reference`, `w3c`, `data_brick` and `snapshot`; the
reference evaluator's library, `reference` and `chaos`; the bench; pytest
787. Also `cargo fmt`, clippy with `-D warnings`, and the wasm32 core.

Times are within the spread everywhere (brick-small1's first after-set is an
outlier the second set does not repeat). On one thread lubm-50's peak fell
from 524 to 463 MB, 53.1 to 47.0 bytes per closure triple: under the budget
of 50 for the first time. On all cores it did not move (599 to 603 MB): the
dictionary's 3.2 bytes per triple went, and the memory the run does not hold
at its end rose by as much. On all cores the peak falls at the end of the run,
when the parallel steps' copies are largest, and memory freed earlier is
reused by them.

Lookups that miss got slower (up to 1.6 times at three quarters full); hits
and new terms got faster, and loading did not move.

## Correction

Iteration 6's reviewer read the per-run peaks instead of the bench's largest of
3 runs. Across 21 all-cores runs of unchanged code, 18 fall within 7 MB of
their median and 3 lie 27 to 47 MB above it. By the median, this iteration
lowered lubm-50's all-cores peak from 629 to 605 MB, 24 MB, which the
comparison above missed. From iteration 6 on, the all-cores peak is judged on
at least 10 runs per side, alternating, by medians and the middle halves of
the per-run peaks.
