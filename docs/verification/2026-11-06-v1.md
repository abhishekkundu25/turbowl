# Verification: version 1's Baseline (2026-11-06)

Version 1 as it stands on `main`, commit `f9e3625`, measured as the
Baseline (`CONTEXT.md`). Version 2's budgets compare against these
numbers, and the phase 1 close-out (`2026-11-06-v2-phase-1.md`) measured
on the same day with the same binary.

- **Speed and memory** ran on a machine busy with the maintainer's other jobs:
  1-minute load average 1.1 to 2.3, not under 1. The maintainer chose to
  measure anyway. The spread between runs stays under 7% on every scenario
  for TurbOWL and under 4% for Nemo. The gaps between the two engines are
  far larger than that, but any claim within a few percent waits for a
  quiet machine.
- **Correctness** is wave 4's record (`2026-11-05-wave-4.md`). This record
  adds the scaling tests of `f9e3625`, which all pass.

Raw results are in [`data/2026-11-06-v1/`](data/2026-11-06-v1/):
`v1-baseline.txt` and `.json` (the bench), and `v1-scaling.txt` (the
scaling tests).

What limits each number:
- **Loading limits TurbOWL's total on LUBM**: 84% of lubm-50's total
  (3.9 of 4.7 s) and 86% of lubm-1's. On the Brick inputs, loading and
  reasoning are about even on brick-buildings, and loading is 76% of
  brick-small1's 12 ms.
- **Memory**: TurbOWL uses 147 to 166 bytes per closure triple on LUBM and 51 on
  brick-buildings. The small inputs are dominated by the fixed cost of the
  process (277 bytes per triple on brick-small1).
- **Nemo** spends its time reasoning. Its load is 9% to 32% of its total.

## Scaling tests

The 26 tests of `f9e3625` that fit how time grows with input size all pass,
run alone at the same load. The highest exponent is 1.18
(`scaling_leapjoin`), under the limit of 1.25. `v1-scaling.txt` has each
test's table.
