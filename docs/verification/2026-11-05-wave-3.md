# Verification: wave 3 (2026-11-05)

The state after wave 3: tickets 01 to 10 at commit `3252f68`, before the
equality rules (ticket 11) and before the project was renamed from
turtle-reasoner to TurbOWL. The raw results call this reasoner
`turtle-reasoner`; the tables here call it TurbOWL.

- **Correctness** ran on the full oracle suite.
- **Speed and memory** ran on a machine busy with the maintainer's other jobs
  (1-minute load average 6.7 to 8.6, not under 1). They show a trend and
  support no claim.
- **The quiet-machine verification** belongs to ticket 14.

Raw results are in [`data/2026-11-05-wave-3/`](data/2026-11-05-wave-3/).

- **MiB:** the largest peak resident set (2^20 bytes).

Three tests check a bound instead of fitting an exponent:

- **`scaling_dictionary_bytes_per_term`:** 73.7 bytes per term from 65,536
  to 1,048,576 terms (16.3 of them text), under the test's bound.
- **`scaling_variable_tiers`:** a variable holds at most 11 stable tiers
  after 65,536 single-tuple batches, against a bound of 17. After
  1,048,576 tuples in batches of 64, it holds at most 10, against 21.
- **`scaling_variable_small_batches_comparisons`:** orderings per tuple
  stay near half of log2(N/b)², a ratio of 0.536 to 0.494 from N = 16,384
  to 262,144. This is the N·log²(N/b) cost of datafrog's design; E10 would
  remove it.
