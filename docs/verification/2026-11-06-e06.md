## What the probe found

The implementer's probe read, at every phase of the run, the process's
footprint, its lifetime peak and the bytes the allocator holds in use
(`malloc_zone_statistics`), on all cores, 5 runs before and 3 after:

- **The program's live bytes never pass the budget.** The most in use at any
  mark is 455.8 to 460.6 MB, in step 3, against 517.3 MB for 50 bytes per
  closure triple. The marks sample it; nothing reads it continuously.
- **The footprint ratchets.** At the end it is 199 to 205 MB above the bytes in
  use: memory the run freed and the allocator keeps. It almost never falls.
- **Where it grows** (base, mean of 5): `start` +34 MB; round 1's rules +84 MB
  while the bytes in use grow by 46; the first index builds of `rdf:type` and
  `takesCourse` +28 and +25 MB; steps 1 to 4 about 120 to 150 MB; step 4's
  index change of `rdf:type`, a fresh 29 MB vector, +28.5 MB.

So the all-cores gap to the budget is mostly the allocator keeping freed
memory after large, short-lived allocations, not copies alive at once. What
the run allocates fresh, and how large, decides the footprint more than what
it holds at any moment.
